How a mandate runs

Five stages, the lifecycle professional interim managers work to, with the exit planned from day one rather than negotiated at the end.

  1. Before day one

    Agree the mandate

    Objectives, KPIs, budget and sign-off authority, the reporting line, and what success looks like at exit, agreed in writing with the sponsor. An interim needs the same authority as the executive they replace.

  2. Week one

    Diagnose and plan

    I meet the team, partners and key customers, read the numbers, and present a written 30, 60 and 90 day plan. If the real problem differs from the brief, I say so early.

  3. First 30 to 60 days

    Stabilize and win early

    Cash, liabilities and urgent risks brought under control, plus visible early wins that build trust with the team and with head office.

  4. Throughout

    Execute and report

    Fortnightly reviews with the sponsor against the agreed KPIs, adjusting priorities as the situation changes. Changing habits on the ground matters as much as fixing processes.

  5. Final month

    Hand over

    I step back to test that the business runs without me, coach or help recruit the permanent leader, document the key processes, and leave the board my recommendations for what comes next.
